Art by Ogden

Watercolor · Insects in Watercolor

Follow new work at @artbyogden

Before 2025, I'd only done a handful of watercolor paintings — small attempts at landscapes and sunsets — until one day it occurred to me to try painting bugs. After all, I'm an evolutionary entomologist by training. Since then, watercolor has become a true passion, and a way to bring my scientific work into closer conversation with my artistic eye.

The Orders: Insects in Watercolor is the first major project to come out of that turn. There are thirty-one orders of insects — the major branches of the insect family tree — and I set out to paint a representative species from each, then assembled them into a book of the same title. All insects share a common ancestor, and over hundreds of millions of years they have diversified into the staggering variety we see today.

There are more insects on Earth than any other kind of animal, but only a handful — butterflies, beetles, bees — get much attention from artists. I want to make space for the rest. From the humble housefly to the majestic mantid, my hope is that these paintings help others see the small, overlooked beauty that surrounds us every day.

About the artist

Heath Ogden is a watercolor painter and evolutionary entomologist based in Orem, Utah. He is a tenured professor and Chair of the Department of Biology at Utah Valley University, where his research uses phylogenetics and bioinformatics to study the evolution of insects, including the origin and evolution of insect flight.

Though art has interested him since high school, his serious watercolor practice began in 2025, when he set out to paint a representative species from every order of insect. That project became The Orders: Insects in Watercolor, a self-published hardcover book and the basis for his first solo exhibition at the Salt Lake City Public Library in spring 2026, with a second solo opening at the Provo City Library in Spring 2027.

He works primarily in watercolor on paper, drawn to the way the medium handles iridescence, translucency, and the fine architectural detail of insect bodies. He is a member of the Utah Watercolor Society.

The Orders: Insects in Watercolor

The Project

There are thirty-one orders of insects — the major branches of the insect family tree. Each order represents a group of creatures that share a common ancestor and a common body plan, shaped by hundreds of millions of years of evolution. The Orders is a painting for every one of them.

A phylogenetic tree of the insect orders accompanies the paintings, showing the evolutionary relationships between groups. Each branch tip is one of the orders. The project began in 2025 and became the basis for solo exhibitions at the Salt Lake City Public Library (2026) and the Provo City Library (March 2027).

The Book

All thirty-one paintings, the phylogenetic tree, and accompanying text are collected in a hardcover book. The first printing of 100 sold out; a second printing of 200 was produced by QinPrinting. Edited by AJ Ogden.
