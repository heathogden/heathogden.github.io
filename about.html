Evolutionary biologist, biology educator, and mayfly enthusiast at Utah Valley University.

I grew up with a deep curiosity about the natural world, which eventually led me to study zoology at BYU. As an undergraduate, I discovered that the real excitement of biology wasn't just in textbooks — it was in the open questions. That realization sent me to Chile for my master's, back to BYU for my PhD, and then to Arizona State University for my postdoctoral work, before joining UVU in 2009.

My research sits at the intersection of insect evolution, phylogenomics, and biology education. I use molecular and morphological data to reconstruct the evolutionary history of mayflies (Ephemeroptera) and other basal winged insects — questions about the origin of flight, the evolution of mayfly morphology, and the forces shaping genome diversity. A second thread of my work focuses on pedagogy: how students learn evolution, what improves acceptance of evolutionary theory, and how teaching methods like flipped classrooms affect learning outcomes.

One of the most rewarding parts of this work has been doing it alongside undergraduate researchers. Every project in this lab has been driven by students — their questions, their effort, and their discoveries. I know firsthand how transformative an undergraduate research experience can be, and providing those opportunities is something I take seriously.

In 2023 I became Chair of the Biology Department at UVU, a role that has added new dimensions to the work — supporting colleagues, building curriculum, and helping the department grow — while keeping my research and my students at the center.
